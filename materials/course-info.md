# Course information

## North-star goal

- At the end of the course, students should be able to frame a data problem, prepare data responsibly, choose and evaluate a suitable machine-learning approach, and explain when distributed data systems are needed.
- The target situation is a realistic analytics project in which data quality, model choice, scale, and processing architecture all matter.
- The platform should make difficult distinctions visible before students meet dense notation or system terminology.
- Strong performance means the student can justify a choice, trace the relevant mechanism, and transfer the idea to a fresh case.

## Course character

- Preferred teaching approach: intuition first, then formal terminology and notation
- Desired balance: short explanations, one worked example, purposeful manipulation or classification, and a fresh transfer check
- Preferred feedback tone: direct, explanatory, and respectful of reasonable wrong answers
- Examples that fit these learners: retail, healthcare monitoring, fraud detection, sensor streams, data warehouses, and practical model pipelines from the decks
- Terminology and notation: preserve lecture terminology while correcting only obvious text-extraction artifacts in learner-facing prose
- Avoid: slide-sized walls of text, decorative click-to-reveal, and unexplained jargon

## Teaching intent

- Role of the platform: course companion and revision environment
- Most important outcomes: reason about learning paradigms and data scale; diagnose data-quality problems; choose transformations; distinguish feature selection, extraction, and reduction; connect later ML methods to distributed systems
- Assessment relationship: formative support only; no grades or attempt limits
- Learner actions: predict, classify, manipulate, compare, diagnose, trace, and explain
- Visible progression: a clear course path with restrained completion feedback and no streaks, points, or timers
- Practice strategy: embedded checks plus integrated practice modules at the end of each implemented lecture sequence
- Practice formats: classification, MCQ with misconception-based distractors, parameter exploration, short calculation, and self-explanation prompts
- Feedback timing and retry policy: immediate explanatory feedback, explicit continuation, answer restoration on revisit, and reset available
